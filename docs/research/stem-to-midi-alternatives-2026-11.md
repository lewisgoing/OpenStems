# Keep the plan, swap its models

**Verdict: keep the plan's design and change what goes into it.** As of 2026-11-25 no other route (a foundation encoder, an audio language model, a diffusion or token model, a mix-to-MIDI model, or any commercial engine) gives more correct MIDI from separated stems than the plan's own shape: measure first (M0), send each stem to a specialist, clean up across stems, then train on the app's own separator output (M6). What the check did find is that several of the plan's model choices and one of its facts are out of date. **Basic Pitch, the plan's default for guitar, keys and "other", is the weakest model in the newest third-party comparison on exactly those stems** (Slakh stems: onset F1 .394, with offsets .163) ([Harmonica](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04640v1)). Guitar and bass note models the plan calls unreleased are now downloadable under an MIT card ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/xavriley/midi-transcription-models)); a new Apache-2.0 multi-instrument transcriber, dettato, exists ([dettato](https://github.com/kobimusic/dettato)); and the plan's bass fallback cannot reach a 5-string's low B ([torchcrepe source](https://github.com/maxrmorrison/torchcrepe/blob/master/torchcrepe/core.py)). Two cheap accuracy methods are missing from the plan: pitch-shift test-time aggregation, and weakly supervised fine-tuning on real audio. Every figure below is *reported*: read from the cited page by a fetch tool and not re-checked against the original table, and none was measured on separated stems, so nothing here replaces M0. The owner has new licence questions to answer, set out at the end.

## No alternative route beats a specialist per stem

The question was whether something fundamentally different would give more correct notes. The evidence says no, and for each route there is a number.

| Route | Why it is ruled out | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audio language models (Qwen Omni, Gemini, Audio Flamingo) | On PitchBench every one scores under 50% note-level F1 on polyphony, and all fall below Basic Pitch overall (best 53.9 against 75.2) | [PitchBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26176) |
| Foundation encoders (MERT, MuQ, MusicFM) as the transcriber's input | In the one direct ablation MERT input scores below a plain mel-spectrogram (onset 48.5 against 52.4). MERT and MuQ weights are CC BY-NC | [MuScriptor](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08168v1), [MERT card](https://huggingface.co/m-a-p/MERT-v1-330M), [MuQ card](https://huggingface.co/OpenMuQ/MuQ-large-msd-iter) |
| Codec tokens as input | EnCodec tokens are the worst of four inputs (onset 39.7 against 52.4 for mel) | [MuScriptor](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08168v1) |
| One large mix-to-MIDI model (MuScriptor) | Weights CC BY-NC 4.0 and gated; and in a third-party run on single stems it scores .457 on Slakh stems and .808 on GuitarSet, below MT3 and YourMT3+ | [muscriptor](https://github.com/muscriptor/muscriptor), [Harmonica](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04640v1) |
| Diffusion for piano (D3RM) | 100 steps to reach onset 97.57, still below the Transkun line's 98.32 | [D3RM](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05068v2) |
| Diffusion for drums (Noise-to-Notes) | Best published drum figures, but no code or weights, and it depends on MERT-330M (non-commercial) | [Noise-to-Notes](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21739v2) |
| A music generator run in reverse | No published transcription result was found; the nearest work only scores generated audio by transcribing it | [spectrogram diffusion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.05408) |
| Symbolic language-model rescoring | Error rate 21.4% to 21.2% even with the best setting picked by oracle; 15 of 60 songs improved | [Hiramatsu et al.](https://eita-nakamura.github.io/articles/Hiramatsu_etal_TranscriptionCorrection_ICASSP2021.pdf) |
| Fine-tuning weights on the user's own song | Self-supervised losses on unlabelled audio from another distribution made predictions "degenerate to blank" | [arXiv 2506.23371](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23371v1) |
| Neural note-level separation (NoteSep) | Under review, no code, and it reallocates mixture energy among concurrent notes, which is the line the plan drew around Celemony's patent (section 10) | [NoteSep](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29071) |
| Released drum models | ADT_STR is CC BY-SA, 289 MB, with cymbal and ride F1 of 0.49 to 0.52 on real kits; ADTOF is non-commercial | [ADT_STR](https://github.com/pier-maker92/ADT_STR), [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09520v1) |
| VocalParse, timbreAMT | VocalParse gives score-level notes with no onset or offset times; timbreAMT is "on par with BasicPitch" | [VocalParse](https://github.com/pymaster17/VocalParse), [timbreAMT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12712v4) |

Two models were not ruled out but cannot be used today. **Harmonica** (BandLab, September 2026) is the direct successor to Basic Pitch: its 26.3K-parameter size scores .731 on Slakh stems against Basic Pitch's .394, and its 679K size .877, but no weights or code were found and its project page did not resolve ([Harmonica](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04640)). SFT-CRNN edges Transkun on piano onsets by 0.04 points with worse offsets and no weights ([SFT-CRNN](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17405v1)). Nothing new and usable was found for lead-vocal notes or for pitch trackers, so the plan's M3 choices stand; that is absence of evidence from general searches, not a dedicated sweep.

The products do not change the picture either. Melodyne is still version 5 and nothing in its history since 5.0 describes a change to detection ([Celemony](https://helpcenter.celemony.com/M5/doc/melodyneStudio5/en/M5tour_VersionHistory)). The only peer-reviewed measurement of its MIDI, at default settings, gives onset F1 0.52 on piano and 0.28 on GuitarSet, and 0.13 or less with offsets ([MT3 paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.03017)). The owner's own higher figures on clean clips (plan, section 7: 0.502 vocals, 0.713 guitar solos, 0.586 guitar chords) are the safer bar, because that paper does not say how it handled Melodyne's export tempo. No DAW was found to pair its new stem separation with polyphonic audio-to-MIDI, and Samplab, the best-known stems-plus-notes product, closed on 2026-09-17 ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/19/apple-samplab-acquisition/)). Drums are weak in every reviewed product ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/music-tech/klang-io-promises-to-turn-audio-into-notated-transcriptions-lead-sheets-and-guitar-tabs-but-does-it-actually-work-klang-io-transcription-studio-review)), which supports the plan's order. No published study measures pitched transcription on a modern separator's output: M0 would still be the first.

## Four new candidates and one fault change the model choices

**The plan's polyphonic default is the weak point.** The plan picked Basic Pitch for guitar, keys and "other" because of its pitch contour (section 4, M4). The Harmonica table, which runs 11 baselines under one protocol on single-instrument audio, puts it last or near last on every set, and at .394 onset F1 on clean Slakh stems, in line with the plan's own 0.42 ([Harmonica](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04640v1)). MT3-class and harmonic-CQT models score about .90 on the same stems. A model that starts at .39 on clean stems has no room left for separation damage. Basic Pitch keeps one thing no newcomer offers, the contour for per-note bends, so the correct split is: another model decides which notes exist, and Basic Pitch's contour or the plan's instantaneous-frequency step (section 5, step 5) supplies the curve.

A caution on that same table: its piano-architecture rows score far below their own papers (Transkun .926 on MAESTRO against .983 in its repository), which suggests they were retrained on mixed-instrument data ([Harmonica](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04640v1), [Transkun](https://github.com/Yujia-Yan/Transkun)). The fetched text did not say. The plan's "Transkun .873 on GuitarSet (third-party)" comes from this table, so it describes the architecture and not necessarily the checkpoint in the wheel. M0 must measure the released files.

**Guitar and bass note models now exist.** `xavriley/midi-transcription-models` holds `guitar-gaps.pth`, `guitar-fl.pth` and `filobass_20000_iterations.pth`, about 99 MB each, under an MIT card ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/xavriley/midi-transcription-models)). They use Kong's CRNN, the class the plan already ports for piano, so each costs one catalog entry. The GAPS paper reports 86.1 to 88.1 onset F1 on GuitarSet zero-shot for this family against 79.0 for Basic Pitch, and 84.8 against 66.1 on a second real-guitar set ([GAPS](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08653v1)). The bass model was trained on Demucs-separated double bass, the app's own input condition, though jazz only and with no published score ([FiloBass](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.02023)). The catch is the data: the GAPS page says the set "may be used only for non-commercial research purposes", and FiloBass's backing tracks are copyrighted ([GAPS page](https://aim-qmul.github.io/GAPS)). The loader's README also calls the models "optimized for monophonic performance", which conflicts with the polyphonic GAPS paper ([loader](https://github.com/xavriley/hf_midi_transcription)).

**dettato is the one new release that fits the licence rules as written.** Code and weights are Apache-2.0, in safetensors; the 15.3M-parameter transcriber is the relevant part, since the app already knows each stem's instrument; a 4-minute song takes about 15 s in under 4 GB on an RTX 5060, and it runs on Apple silicon ([dettato](https://github.com/kobimusic/dettato)). Its author reports Slakh test onset F1 73.5 and a composite of 58.4 against 44.6 for MuScriptor and 41.1 for YourMT3+ ([model card](https://huggingface.co/kobimusic/dettato)). Those are the author's numbers, from a 13-commit repository, on full mixes only; its pitches are limited to 88 keys with at most two notes per key. It replaces MR-MT3 as the plan's clean multi-instrument baseline if M0 confirms it.

**tsumugi is the closest in intent and cannot ship.** It offers per-stem checkpoints for bass, guitar, vocal, vocal harmony, other and drums, built to run after a separator, with MIT code, CUDA 13.0 on Windows and MPS, but states no licence for its weights and does not disclose its training data ([tsumugi](https://github.com/anime-song/instrument-agnostic-amt)). Under the plan's rules that is tier C: a reference in M0 and nothing more.

**The bass fallback is wrong as written.** M3 sends notes below SwiftF0's 46.9 Hz floor to torchcrepe or pYIN. torchcrepe's `predict` defaults to `fmin=50.` and its lowest bin is about 31.7 Hz, so it misses low E at its defaults and cannot represent a 5-string's low B (30.9 Hz) at all ([torchcrepe source](https://github.com/maxrmorrison/torchcrepe/blob/master/torchcrepe/core.py)). pYIN, PENN (MIT, floor 31 Hz) and the note models whose bins start at A0 (Basic Pitch, and the Kong class by its 88 keys) reach lower ([PENN](https://github.com/interactiveaudiolab/penn), [Basic Pitch constants](https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch/blob/main/basic_pitch/constants.py)). No published accuracy below 50 Hz was found for any tracker, and the plan's Slakh bass set has no note below G1.

## What changes, stage by stage

M1 needs no change. The rest:

| Stage | Change | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M0 | Add to the candidates: dettato's transcriber; `guitar-gaps.pth`, `guitar-fl.pth` and `filobass` (after the plan's pickle inspection); tsumugi as an evaluation-only reference. Check HPPNet-sp's repository for weights (1.2M parameters, strong out of domain in the Harmonica table) | Above |
| M0 | Measure released checkpoints, not table rows: the Transkun and Basic Pitch "third-party" figures in section 4 come from a table whose baselines look retrained | [Harmonica](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04640v1) |
| M0 | Add a pitch-shift aggregation row (TAPS) for each posterior model: transcribe shifted copies, shift back, and average only where the model is unsure. Reported +3.3 on GuitarSet and +3.4 on MAPS under domain shift, with a 0.6 loss in domain | [TAPS](https://apmcleod.github.io/pdf/icassp-taps.pdf) |
| M0 | Add a bass set that reaches 30 Hz (the synthetic synth extended down) and real bass through the separator | Above |
| M0 | Add one drum row: learned whole-kit onsets (ADTOF, evaluation only) with DrumSep parts used for class and velocity, against per-part onsets | [arXiv 2509.24853](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24853v1) |
| M0 | Add Klangio and AnthemScore exports to the baseline pack: they hold the best commercial polyphonic figures (vendor-run: raw note F1 0.82 and 0.76) | [Klangio benchmark](https://klang.io/blog/music-transcription-benchmark-2026/) |
| M0 | Stop treating YourMT3+ as the guitar leader. In the Harmonica table it sits at .889 on GuitarSet, within two points of MT3, SFT-CRNN and Harmonica; the case for carrying GPL code is weaker | [Harmonica](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04640v1) |
| M2a | Use the published rules: a hi-hat is open if loudness stays above 75% of its maximum until the next hit (at most 150 ms); crash or ride by the louder separated part. Normalise velocity per part, since an equal-loudness filter across the kit skews the snare high | [arXiv 2509.24853](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24853v1) |
| M2b | Add STAR Drums (124.5 h, 18 classes with open, closed and pedal hi-hat, crash, ride and ride bell) to the training data once its licence is read; train on many kits (1 kit instead of 20 costs 21.5% relative); add a velocity head trained where velocity is real (E-GMD) | [STAR Drums](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/10.5334/tismir.244), [Weyers et al.](https://zenodo.org/records/17706523), [Noise-to-Notes](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21739v2) |
| M2b | Expect the gain from parts to be about overlap: a small CRNN scores 0.91 on drums-only audio and 0.98 when no two hits fall within 50 ms. That is the ceiling perfect separation offers, and it matches the plan's missed hi-hats and ride | [Weyers et al.](https://zenodo.org/records/17706523) |
| M2b | Test pretrained-encoder features for carrying an E-GMD-trained model over to recorded kits: MERT features added about 11 points on MDB in one ablation. MERT itself is non-commercial, so the candidate is the app's own separator encoder (the plan's idea 10) | [Noise-to-Notes](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21739v2) |
| M3 | Fix the low range: call torchcrepe with `fmin` near 30 Hz or drop it; for notes below about 50 Hz choose in M0 among pYIN, PENN and a note model from A0 | Above |
| M4 | Choose the note model for guitar, keys and "other" from M0, with Basic Pitch no longer the presumed default; keep its contour, or instantaneous frequency, for bends | Above |
| M4 | Move "test-time adaptation" here from M7 as TAPS with frozen weights, if M0 shows a gain on separated stems | [TAPS](https://apmcleod.github.io/pdf/icassp-taps.pdf) |
| M4 | Take velocity from amplitude for any GAPS-trained guitar model: GAPS labels are a constant 100 | [arXiv 2606.24912](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.24912) |
| M4 | Use the beat grid and key as evidence that a note exists, never to move it. Decoding on a 16th-note grid raised note F1 from 58.1 to 65.9 with a given grid, but part of that is the metric rewarding quantised output | [Ycart et al.](https://zenodo.org/records/3527842/files/ismir2019_paper_000054.pdf) |
| M5 | Draw the hollow notes from the model's unsure band. In TAPS's data 99.4% of outputs were within 0.2 of 0 or 1 and 98.9% of those correct, while unsure ones were right 57.9% of the time | [TAPS](https://apmcleod.github.io/pdf/icassp-taps.pdf) |
| M6 | Compare a harmonic-CQT CNN of 0.7M to 3M parameters against the Edwards/Kong start for guitar and "other": that family leads the Harmonica table on non-piano stems | [Harmonica](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04640v1) |
| M6 | Add pitch-shifted copies with shifted labels (GuitarSet 75.4 to 82.9 in NoteEM) and amp-rendered guitar for distortion (a MAESTRO-trained model falls to 42.6 on high gain) | [NoteEM](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.13668), [arXiv 2504.07406](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07406) |
| M6 | Add weakly supervised fine-tuning on real separated stems with unaligned or count-only labels: a synthetic-pretrained model reached 85.8 to 86.5 on GuitarSet from note counts alone, trained on two RTX 3090s. Subject to the owner's decision below | [CountEM](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.14250v1) |
| M6 | Try a small learned refiner over the base model's piano roll (D3RM's denoiser is 477K parameters and adds 2.8 offset points over its own baseline) | [D3RM](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05068v2) |
| M7 | Drop symbolic language-model rescoring and per-song weight adaptation. Keep resynthesis as a per-stem quality score, not a per-note check: a learned similarity between rendered MIDI and the recording ranked piano systems as listeners did (Spearman 0.971) | [arXiv 2608.04511](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04511) |

Two of the plan's export defaults gain support and stay: plain notes with bends as an option, since excess or out-of-tune bends are the most-cited reason an export was marked down, and the real tempo in the file, where NeuralNote exports at 120 BPM ([Futureproof](https://futureproofmusicschool.com/blog/best-audio-to-midi-converters)). That source is an aggregator of forum threads, not a test.

## What must be measured, and what the owner must decide

**Nothing in this report was measured on the app's separated stems.** Every gain above comes from clean recordings or full mixes. Before any of it is relied on, the M0 benchmark must show, on clean stems, separated stems and the mix: the guitar and bass checkpoints against Basic Pitch, Kong/Edwards and Transkun, including on chords, where the README's "monophonic" remark is unexplained; dettato's transcriber on single stems, where its author gives no figure; pitch-shift aggregation on models other than Onsets and Frames, which is all its paper tested; every low-bass option below 50 Hz; whether per-part drum onsets beat whole-kit onsets, which no paper compares; and whether the unsure band really holds the errors for the model that ships. On the Mac, each new model is unverified on MPS until the owner runs it, and every Mac memory figure remains an estimate. No paper reports timings on a 16 GB consumer card, an M1 Max or a CPU for any model here except dettato's own README figures.

Several facts in the notes are also unconfirmed and should be read at source before they steer a decision: which Harmonica baselines were retrained; dettato's LICENSE and NOTICE files, its soundfont licences and the Creative Commons variant it claims for URMP; STAR Drums' licence identifier on Zenodo; the conflict between the GAPS page's non-commercial terms and a CC BY 4.0 mention on Zenodo; and tsumugi's Hugging Face card. The ISMIR 2026 proceedings were not searched directly, and a KAIST paper on piano transcription in ensemble settings, the closest conference work to a stem with bleed in it, is unread ([KAIST publications](https://mac.kaist.ac.kr/pubs.html)).

## Conclusion

The check changes where the plan's risk sits. Before it, the risk was that a better design existed elsewhere. It does not: the strongest 2026 systems win through private data, and the one architecture that clearly supersedes the plan's default has no weights. The risk is now inside M4, in a default chosen for its bend curves that scores worst at finding notes, and for an owner who puts correct MIDI first that order is backwards. Note detection and pitch curves should be two jobs done by two components.

The second shift is in what M6 is for. The published models that do well on stems are small, a few hundred thousand to a few million parameters, and were trained on isolated stems; the app can make unlimited separated-stem audio with exact labels from CC BY multitracks. That makes the app's own training the most direct route to MIDI that is both correct and cleanly licensed, and the downloadable guitar and bass checkpoints become the score its own model has to beat. M0 remains the gate for all of it, and it is still a measurement nobody has published.
