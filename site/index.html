A desktop app for producers. Drop files, folders or a link, pick a recipe, and every song comes back as stems exactly as long as the source, so they line up at bar 1 in your DAW.

download 0.4.0 source on github

01 how it works

How it works

  1. dropFiles, folders or a pasted link. They queue with the recipe, quality and format you pick.
  2. separateOne GPU worker runs the queue and keeps the last model warm. Progress is live.
  3. drag outSolo, mute, then drag one stem, all of them, or a mix straight into the DAW.

02

What it does

f.01

Preview before you export

Each stem gets a waveform with solo and mute, and they all play in sync. Tempo and key are detected per song.

f.02

Split drums into the kit

The drum-kit recipe pulls out the drums, then splits them into kick, snare, toms, hi-hat, ride and crash.

f.03

Export a mix

Mute or solo stems and drag out what you hear as a single file, e.g. the song without vocals.

f.04

Cut parts on a grid

Select a section that snaps to bars and beats, correct the grid if detection is off, and drag the clip out.

f.05

Tempo sync for FL Studio

WAV stems can carry the detected tempo. FL Studio stretches short clips automatically; for a full stem, use Fit to tempo in the clip menu.

f.06

Separate from a link

Paste a URL and it is downloaded and queued like a file. The tray (menu bar on a Mac) window accepts links and files too.

Opening it the first time

The app is signed with the project's own certificate, not an Apple Developer ID, so macOS will not open it on a double click.

  1. Double-click OpenStems. macOS says it could not verify the app. Click Done.
  2. Open System Settings and click Privacy & Security.
  3. Under Security, OpenStems is named as blocked: click Open Anyway (there for about an hour after step 1).
  4. Confirm, and enter your login password if asked.

If macOS says it "is damaged", move it to Applications and run this in Terminal:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/OpenStems.app
